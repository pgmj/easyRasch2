# Impact-calibrated tolerance for conditional infit

Design note written 2026-10-12. Nothing has been run. It builds on

- `conditional_rmsd_partA.qmd`: infit MSQ is an n-stable effect size, the
  cascade is a population effect, and no single fixed band separates
  misfitting items from cascade-affected ones across conditions;
- `fwb_msq_coverage.qmd`: FWB intervals for the observed MSQ have the right
  spread and close to nominal coverage, and a floor of $\pm .10$ stops the
  cascade;
- the deferred infit impact plan (memory `easyrasch2-infit-impact-sensitivity-plan`)
  and the DIF impact function (`dif-impact-design.md`), which ask the same
  keep, remove or model question for DIF.

## 1. The quantity to calibrate

An effect-size floor judges item $i$ by its FWB interval for the population
MSQ against a band $[1 - \delta_i^-, 1 + \delta_i^+]$. This note defines the
band from consequences. The user states a tolerance $\tau$ on an impact
metric in score units. $\delta_i^-$ and $\delta_i^+$ are the departures of the
MSQ at which item $i$'s misfit reaches that tolerance. The band is item
specific because the same misfit shows less in the MSQ of an off-target item
and also affects fewer persons. It is asymmetric because underfit and overfit
have different consequences.

## 2. Reference: the same item, fitting

Impact is defined against a **counterfactual**, not against removing items.
For item $i$ and misfit magnitude $m$, two datasets share every person and
every response to the other items:

- **(a) misfitting:** item $i$ generated with misfit of size $m$;
- **(b) fitting:** item $i$ generated by the Rasch model at the same location,
  with the same uniform draws (common random numbers), so (a) and (b) differ
  only through the misfit.

Each is analysed as a user would: CML refit, then person estimates. Impact of
the misfit is the difference between (a) and (b), each judged against the
true $\theta$.

**Why not a random-removal baseline.** Sinharay and Haberman (2014) compare
removing the misfitting items with removing the same number of random items,
to separate the effect of misfit from the effect of a shorter test. In real
data that comparison assumes the other items fit and are locally
independent. If they are not, removing a random item mixes in their problems.
The counterfactual in (b) needs no such assumption. Length is the same in
(a) and (b), and in the simulation the other items fit by construction. It
measures what the misfit costs, holding everything else fixed.

**Removal is a separate decision.** A third dataset, **(c) item $i$
removed**, also judged against the true $\theta$, answers whether to keep the
item at all: an underfitting item can still improve measurement of the
intended trait. In the linear-composite theory (memory note), keeping beats
removing above a relative slope of about 0.5, or a correlation with the
second dimension of about .72. The comparison (a) against (c) gives the
keep-or-remove advice. The floor is set from (a) against (b).

## 3. Misfit mechanisms

The MSQ is a symptom, so the calibration needs a generating mechanism. Two,
reported side by side:

- **M1, slope.** Item $i$ has discrimination $a$ relative to the Rasch common
  slope (GPCM for polytomous items). $a < 1$ gives underfit, $a > 1$ overfit.
  Grid: $a \in \{0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 1, 1.15, 1.3, 1.5, 1.75, 2\}$.
- **M2, second dimension.** Item $i$ responds to $\theta_2$, correlated $\rho$
  with $\theta_1$, same SD. Underfit only. Grid:
  $\rho \in \{.3, .5, .6, .7, .8, .85, .9, .95, 1\}$.

M2 is the mechanism used in Part A, the S-X2 studies and the FWB check. M1 is
the parametric counterpart of the infit identity (infit is a slope contrast
for dichotomous items). If the two give clearly different $\delta$ for the
same item, report the range rather than choosing.

**The intended trait under M2 is $\theta_1$**, the trait the other items
measure. Impact is measured against $\theta_1$.

## 4. Persons and estimation

- **Persons.** Normal with the mean and SD estimated from the data (MML,
  `.estimate_prior_sd()`, mean fixed as in 1.3.1). A resampled-WLE option can
  follow if normality matters.
- **Items.** The CML thresholds of the real data for every item, including
  item $i$'s location.
- **Person estimates.** WLE with SE, as the package reports. Also the **sum
  score**, because public health surveys usually report sum scores. Impact
  on the sum score is judged by its rank correlation with $\theta_1$ and by
  group differences.

## 5. Impact metrics

All per person, relative to the true $\theta_1$, then averaged.

| | Metric | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| A | RMSE of $\hat\theta - \theta$, ratio (a)/(b) | Loss of accuracy from the misfit |
| B | $|\hat\theta_a - \hat\theta_b| / \text{SE}_b$: mean, and share above 1 | How far individual scores move, in SEM units |
| C | KL divergence between $N(\hat\theta_a, \text{SE}_a^2)$ and $N(\hat\theta_b, \text{SE}_b^2)$, mean over persons | Location shift and precision change in one number |
| D | $\text{SD}(\hat\theta - \theta) / \text{RMS}(\text{SE})$, (a) against (b) | Whether reported SEs stay honest (overfit makes them too small) |
| E | Group difference in $\hat\theta$ when two groups differ on $\theta_2$ by $d$ with equal $\theta_1$, M2 only | Spurious group differences, in SD units |
| F | $\max_\theta |E(\hat\theta_a - \theta \mid \theta)|$ | Conditional bias, where it is worst |

**KL in more detail.** With normal approximations,

$$
\text{KL} = \tfrac12\Big(\frac{\hat\theta_a - \hat\theta_b}{\text{SE}_b}\Big)^2
+ \tfrac12\Big(r - 1 - \ln r\Big), \qquad r = \text{SE}_a^2 / \text{SE}_b^2 .
$$

The first term is half the squared shift in SEM units, the continuous form of
metric B. The second is zero when the SEs agree and grows when precision
changes. A mean shift of 0.25 SEM with unchanged SEs gives KL = .031, and 0.5
SEM gives .125. KL is asymmetric. The symmetric Jeffreys divergence (sum of
both directions) or the Hellinger distance are alternatives, and reporting
both terms separately keeps the result readable. KL has no direction, so it
cannot show which group a bias favours. That is metric E's job.

A KL between the predicted response probabilities of two models is not used
here. That measures the size of the misfit, not its consequences.

## 6. The calibration loop

For item $i$, mechanism M1 or M2, and each magnitude $m$ on the grid:

1. Generate datasets (a), (b) and (c) at a large n (a population proxy, for
   example 20,000, or the mean over several replicates at 5,000).
2. Record the population MSQ of item $i$ in (a), and the impact metrics.
3. Smooth impact against MSQ (monotone spline), separately below and above 1.
4. Invert at the user's tolerance $\tau$: $\delta_i^-$ is $1 -$ the MSQ below 1
   at which impact reaches $\tau$, and $\delta_i^+$ likewise above 1.

The output per item is a band, the impact curve it came from, and the
keep-or-remove comparison from (c). The band then goes to the FWB floor
from `fwb_msq_coverage.qmd`.

**Cost.** For 20 items, two mechanisms and about 20 magnitudes, each with
three CML fits and WLE at n = 20,000, the calibration of one item takes
roughly 1 to 2 minutes on 10 cores. All items would take about half an hour.
That is too slow for an interactive function unless the grid is coarse or
only flagged items are calibrated. Calibrating only items whose FWB interval
does not already sit well inside a generous band would keep it practical.

## 7. Tolerance $\tau$

The user's choice, stated in score units. Candidates to offer, not defaults:

- metric A: RMSE no more than 2.5 or 5 percent larger than with a fitting
  item;
- metric B: mean shift below 0.25 SEM, or under 5 percent of persons moving
  more than 1 SEM;
- metric C: mean KL below .03 (about a 0.25 SEM shift);
- metric E: spurious group difference below 0.1 SD per unit $d$.

The choice depends on how scores are used: individual decisions, group
comparisons or research means.

## 8. A validation study before any package code

1. **Designs.** The 20-item dichotomous design of Part A (items at 0, -1 and
   -2 logits as calibration targets), and phq9 as a polytomous case (9 items
   with 4 categories).
2. **Checks.**
   - Impact rises monotonically with |MSQ − 1| within each mechanism, so the
     inversion is well defined.
   - M1 and M2 agree, or the size of their disagreement is known.
   - The bands depend on targeting as expected (wider for off-target items).
   - The bands separate Part A's misfitting items from its cascade-affected
     items. If an impact-calibrated band flags the cascade items, the
     calibration or the counterfactual is wrong.
   - A small FWB floor run with the calibrated bands, on Part A datasets.
3. **Cost.** About 1 to 2 hours on 10 cores for both designs and both
   mechanisms at three targeting positions.

## 9. Decisions

Taken by the user on 2026-10-12:

1. **Impact metrics:** A (accuracy), B (score movement), C (KL), E (group
   bias) and F (worst conditional bias), reported together. F was dropped
   and then added back on 2026-10-12, because scores are often used against
   cut-points. Metric D is dropped.
2. **Mechanism:** calibrate on M2 (second dimension). M1 is not run. M2
   produces underfit only (MSQ above 1), so it calibrates the upper side of
   the band, $\delta_i^+$. The lower side, $\delta_i^-$ (overfit, MSQ below 1),
   needs a separate answer later, for example from M1 with slopes above 1.
   This matters for the cascade: cascade-affected items show *overfit*
   (MSQ about 0.92 in Part A), so whether the floor clears them depends on
   $\delta_i^-$, which M2 does not provide. (Corrected 2026-10-12: an earlier
   version of this item had the two sides swapped.)
3. **Person distribution:** resampled WLE from the data, as in the
   `dgp = "resample"` cutoffs. Under M2, $\theta_2 = \rho\,\theta_1 +
   \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\, e$, with $e$ resampled from the same WLE values,
   independently, so that $\theta_2$ has the same distribution shape. WLE
   values are slightly overdispersed relative to the true distribution, the
   same caveat as the resample cutoffs.
4. **Person scores:** WLE as the primary target, and the sum score alongside
   it, since it costs nothing to compute. With complete data, the WLE under
   the Rasch model is a monotone function of the sum score, so the two rank
   persons identically and the rank-based part of metric A is the same for
   both. They differ in metric E, because the WLE stretches the score scale
   at the extremes, so a group difference in SD units is not the same on the
   two scales. Between datasets (a) and (b) the sum scores differ only by
   item $i$'s response, so metrics B and C are not defined for the sum score.
   For the sum score: Spearman correlation with $\theta_1$ (A) and the
   spurious group difference in sum-score SD units (E). With missing
   responses the two scores no longer rank persons identically, and the sum
   score is then harder to interpret.
5. **Calibration runs per dataset**, inside a function, for the items that
   are not already clearly inside a generous band. Same approach as
   `RMitemInfitCutoff()`.

## 10. Relation to RMdifTree

`RMdifTree()` already has both halves of this design for DIF:

- **Purification** (`purification = "iterative"`) drops DIF-flagged items
  from the matching score and recomputes the effect sizes. DIF items
  contaminate the matching score in the same way that misfitting items
  contaminate the total score behind conditional infit. Purification
  removes the contamination from the statistic. The floor tolerates what
  is left. The infit analogue of purification would be conditional infit
  for the remaining items, conditional on a score that excludes the flagged
  items (computable from the ESF of the clean items, if those fit). It
  would move the cascade-affected items' MSQ back toward 1 before the floor
  is applied. Part A shows the floor alone already separates them, so
  purified infit is optional, but it would narrow the undecided zone.
- **ETS A/B/C** is a floor with fixed tolerances. C requires |Δ| ≥ 1.5 and a
  test showing |Δ| > 1.0, which is the minimum-effect test proposed here for
  infit. Its boundaries (1.0 and 1.5 on the MH Delta scale, .21 and .31 for
  partial gamma) are conventions, not impact-based. The DIF impact function
  (`dif-impact-design.md`) asks to replace them with consequences for person
  scores, which is what this note does for infit. The two should share the
  simulation engine and the impact metrics.
